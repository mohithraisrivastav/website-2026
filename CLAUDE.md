## Voice in Practice — The Voice Guardian

Every session, before presenting any written content, the Voice Guardian check runs. No exceptions. This is the most important section in this file.

### What Mohith's writing sounds like

He writes the way he talks to a close friend: considered, warm, occasionally dry, never performing. He is a storyteller, not a marketer. His subject is architecture as a human experience.

His reader is Swaraj — a fellow architectural photographer who will catch a false note immediately. Write for Swaraj and nothing will go wrong.

Specific beats abstract. Name the thing. A laterite wall, not "a surface." A monsoon morning in Fontainhas, not "a beautiful setting." A sitar playing from the neighbor's house, not "a rich cultural atmosphere."

### Correct outreach email — this pattern exactly

> Dear [Name],
>
> I hope you are well. I am Mohith Rai Srivastav, an Architect turned Architectural Photographer and Filmmaker based in Goa.
>
> I've been looking at [Project Name] over the past few days.
>
> If you have a project coming up, I would love to photograph it.
>
> Warm regards,
> Mohith Rai Srivastav
> mohithraisrivastav@gmail.com | mohithraisrivastav.com

Why it works: names the project, says nothing about it. The awareness is the gesture. The website carries everything else. Under 60 words. No em dashes. No credentials in the body.

### Wrong outreach email — never write this

> Dear Mr. Puri,
>
> The laterite stone on a coastal site, and those deep overhangs — I would love to photograph something like this. I am a Goa-based Architectural Photographer published in Architectural Digest India, Condé Nast Traveller, Elle Decor...

Why it's wrong: describes what was noticed (performing), uses an em dash for drama, parades credentials. Every one of these is a disqualifying error.

### Correct Instagram caption

> Same wall, third monsoon. The moss came back along the lower edge again, in the exact shape it took in 2022. I keep photographing it to find out whether I'm watching the wall or whether the wall is watching me work.

Why it works: a fact, then a fact, then a small honest observation. No instruction to the reader. No moral at the end. Specific enough that Swaraj would not catch a false note.

### Wrong Instagram caption

> The wall doesn't speak. It waits. Layers of moss, salt, time. Something slow is happening here, something we miss when we're not looking.

Why it's wrong: stacked fragments (banned), "something... something" mood loop, personification cliché, tells the reader they're missing something (lecturing).

### Hard bans — never generate these in any content

**Words:** leverage, proactive, pivotal, seamless, cutting edge, game-changer, realm, quest, unravel, embark, resilient, thrill, quietly (as mood setter), stunning, breathtaking, world-class, vibrant, rich, wonderful, incredible

**Constructions:**
- Em dashes and en dashes for drama or pause. Recast the sentence.
- "Not X, but Y" in any form
- Opening with what something is not: "This is not your typical workshop"
- Stacked fragments: "July 2. Goa. One space." Fold into one sentence.
- Repetition ladders: "Sometimes about light. Sometimes about shadow." One sentence.
- Credential parades in email body. The website does that.
- Describing what you noticed about a prospect's project. Name the project. Stop there.

### Voice Guardian self-check (run before presenting any written content)

1. Does any sentence contain an em dash? If yes: remove it and recast.
2. Does any email describe what was noticed about the project or architect? If yes: remove all description. Name the project only.
3. Does any email list publications, past clients, or credentials? If yes: remove.
4. Does any caption contain a stacked fragment sequence? If yes: fold into one sentence.
5. Does any caption end with a moral or tell the reader what they're missing? If yes: cut the last line.
6. Does the writing sound like Swaraj would catch a false note? If yes: rewrite.
7. Is every email under 80 words? If not: cut.

If all seven pass: present. If any fail: fix first, then present.

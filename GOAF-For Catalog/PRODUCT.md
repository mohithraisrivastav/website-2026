# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Architects, interior designers, hospitality and real estate developers, editorial art directors, and fine art collectors — typically urban professionals in India (Goa, Mumbai, Bangalore, Delhi) with discerning visual taste. They arrive either via referral, editorial feature, or direct search for an architectural photographer in Goa. Secondary audience: aspiring photographers and architecture students considering Mohith's creative workshops.

## Product Purpose

Portfolio and studio website for Mohith Rai Srivastav — architectural photographer based in Panaji, Goa. The site sells three things simultaneously: commissioned photography services, fine art prints (giclée, limited edition), and creative workshops. Design IS the product: a client's first impression of Mohith's visual intelligence comes from the site itself. Success means a serious prospect closes the browser thinking "this person understands space and light at a level I haven't seen elsewhere."

## Brand Personality

Unhurried. Precise. Earned.

Voice is quiet authority — never promotional, never apologetic. The photographer has exhibited at the Louvre, published in Architectural Digest and Condé Nast Traveller, and taught at international conventions. The site does not announce this; it assumes it. Copy is spare. Negative space is used deliberately. Colour is restrained. The single accent — a warm architectural gold (#CFA246) — is used for moments of focus, never decoration.

## Anti-references

- Behance/Dribbble portfolio clichés (full-bleed hero with name in giant sans-serif, project cards with hover overlays)
- Stock photography agencies (Getty, Shutterstock aesthetic — clinical white, catalogue grids)
- Generic "creative agency" templates (colourful gradients, big emoji, playful motion)
- SaaS product sites repurposed as portfolios (dark mode with glowing CTAs, hero-metric layouts)
- Overly editorial magazine sites where the grid system overshadows the photographs

## Design Principles

1. **The photograph is the hierarchy.** Every typographic and layout decision defers to the image. Text is infrastructure; images are architecture.
2. **Restraint as signal.** Sparse use of gold, limited animation, and deliberate negative space communicate taste. More is cheaper.
3. **Authority without announcement.** Credentials, exhibitions, and publications appear once and accurately. No repetition, no emphasis, no hero stats.
4. **Precision in small things.** Touch targets, spacing rhythm, and typographic scale are exactly right — not approximately right. The same care that goes into a photograph.
5. **The site should feel like the work.** Architectural photography is about light, materiality, and geometry. The site palette, typography, and motion should feel like walking through a considered space.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as the floor. Keyboard navigation supported throughout (hamburger, dropdowns, cart, modals). ARIA roles and aria-labels in place for all interactive non-button elements. Focus indicators preserved. Alt text on all images. Reduced motion not explicitly handled yet — this is a gap.
